Before the recipes

This was a choice.
For a lot of people, it isn't.

Rafael's 30-day budget project was a personal experiment. For 37 million Americans, it's just called Tuesday.

This recipe book started as a challenge - one meal a day, $10 budget cap, no calling mom. A fun constraint. But somewhere around day 12, ramen brain kicked in, and it started feeling less like a project and more like a glimpse into something real. The difference between doing this on purpose and doing this because you have to is everything.

"Food insecurity isn't just about being hungry. It's about the mental load of calculating every purchase, the shame of choosing the cheapest thing on the menu, the exhaustion of making something out of nothing - every single day."

The math is brutal. SNAP benefits (food stamps) average about $6 per person per day in the U.S. That's not $6 per meal - that's $6 for the entire day. The $10/meal cap in this project would feel like luxury. Millions of households make it work on far less, stretching rice and dried beans into a week's worth of food, skipping meals so their kids don't have to, choosing between groceries and rent.

It affects everything downstream. Kids who don't have consistent access to food have worse outcomes in school - concentration, attendance, test scores all take hits. Adults dealing with food stress carry a cognitive load that affects work performance, decision-making, and mental health. Communities with high food insecurity often also lack nearby grocery stores (food deserts), leaving fast food and corner stores as the only options - which cost more and provide less nutrition. The budget cooking skills in this book become essential survival knowledge, not a fun weekend project.

The stigma makes it worse. There's a particular kind of shame that comes with not having enough - the feeling of being judged at the checkout line, the embarrassment of knowing your groceries have a hard ceiling, the way "budget recipes" are sometimes framed as failure rather than resourcefulness. But cheap cooking, done right, is genuinely one of the most skilled things a person can do in a kitchen. Rafael figured that out in 30 days. A lot of people have known it their whole lives.

The recipes in this book aren't charity. They're practical, honest, and actually good. But it felt wrong to collect 30 days of budget cooking without saying: the real people doing this every day deserve respect, resources, and a system that doesn't make feeding yourself and your family feel like a test you might fail.

If you want to help: Feeding America (feedingamerica.org) · local food banks (findhelp.org) · SNAP enrollment assistance (benefits.gov) - these organizations stretch every dollar donated further than you'd expect.

Recipe 01

Japanese Curry Rice with Egg Hat

the one that started it all

Real

"First day of my project, I got hungry for some Japanese food. Sushi is a bit too expensive, so opted for curry instead... I boiled the rice and added the curry paste, then fried an egg sunny-side up style for a hat."

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice (big bag - saves money long term)
  • 2 Japanese curry squares (S&B or House brand)
  • 1 potato, cubed
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • ½ onion, diced
  • 1½ cups water
  • 1 egg (the hat)
  • Oil, salt

Steps

  1. Rinse rice until the water runs clear - this removes excess starch and makes it fluffy instead of gluey. Cook using the absorption method: 1 cup rice, 1.5 cups water, lid on, medium heat until it boils, then lowest heat 12 min, then steam off heat 10 min. Do not lift the lid early. Just don't.
  2. In a separate pot, sauté onion in a little oil until soft and slightly golden, about 5 min. Add carrots and potato, stir for 2 min.
  3. Add 1½ cups water and bring to a boil. Simmer 10-15 min until the potato is fork-tender.
  4. Turn off the heat. Drop in the curry squares and stir until completely dissolved. The sauce will thicken as it cools - this is correct and beautiful.
  5. Fry the egg sunny-side up: medium heat, small puddle of oil, crack egg in gently, cover and cook 2 min. The whites should be fully set, the yolk runny and bright.
  6. Plate the rice, ladle curry alongside it, and place the egg directly on top of the rice. The egg is the hat. The hat is important. Do not skip the hat.
Curry squares are genius budget food: One box costs about $3 and makes 4-6 servings. They keep forever in the pantry. The potato and carrot are basically optional decoration - the curry is good with just rice.
~$4-6 · serves 2, or 1 very hungry person
Rice · Day 1 energy

Recipe 02

Steak & Salsa Pita

with "weirdly grilled" bell peppers

Real

"I decided to get some (cheap) steak... My mother wants me to eat veggies so I have some weirdly grilled bell peppers."

Ingredients

  • 1 cheap steak (skirt, flank, or sirloin tip)
  • 2 pita breads
  • 1 bell pepper, sliced into strips
  • Salsa (jarred - the whole jar if you want)
  • Salt, pepper, garlic powder
  • Oil
  • Sour cream or Greek yogurt (optional)

Steps

  1. Take the steak out of the fridge 15 min before cooking. Season generously with salt, pepper, and garlic powder on both sides. Don't be shy - cheap cuts need seasoning to shine.
  2. Get a pan screaming hot. Add a tiny bit of oil. Sear the steak 2-3 min per side without touching it - let the crust form. For thin cuts like skirt steak, this is basically the whole cook time.
  3. Rest the steak on a cutting board for 5 min. This is not optional. Cut into it immediately and all the juice falls out onto the board and you'll be sad.
  4. In the same hot pan, add the bell pepper strips. Don't stir them - let them sit until they blister and char in spots, 3-4 min. Flip once. This is the "weird grilling" effect. It is delicious.
  5. Slice the steak thin, against the grain. Warm the pita 30 sec in the dry pan. Load with steak, peppers, and a very generous amount of salsa.
Against the grain: Look at the lines running across the steak - slice perpendicular to those lines. This cuts through the muscle fibers instead of along them, making the meat way more tender. Slicing with the grain = chewy. Against = not chewy.
~$8-10 · serves 2
Steak · Feeling rich

Recipe 03

Garlic Parmesan Pasta with Shrimp

wrong pasta shape, zero regrets

Real

"In retrospect, I probably should have chosen spaghetti or any other thicker kind of pasta, but it's okay... It is very filling, and now I wanna go to sleep."

Ingredients

  • 2 portions pasta (any shape - Rafael used the wrong one and survived)
  • Small shrimp, a handful (frozen is fine)
  • 4 cloves garlic, sliced thin
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • ¼ cup parmesan, grated
  • Salt & black pepper
  • Pasta water (save it - this is important)
  • Parsley or chili flakes (optional)

Steps

  1. Boil pasta in heavily salted water. Before draining, save a full cup of pasta water. This starchy water is worth its weight in gold for this sauce.
  2. If using frozen shrimp, thaw under cold running water for 5 min. Pat completely dry with paper towel - wet shrimp steams instead of searing, and steamed shrimp is sad shrimp.
  3. Melt 2 tbsp butter in a pan on medium. Add garlic slices and cook slowly, stirring, until they're pale golden and fragrant - about 3 min. The second they go dark brown, they're bitter. Watch them closely.
  4. Turn heat to medium-high. Add shrimp in a single layer. Cook 1-2 min per side until pink and just curled. Remove shrimp from pan before they overcook (shrimp overcook in seconds - respect this).
  5. Add drained pasta to the garlicky butter pan. Toss. Add parmesan and a big splash of pasta water. Toss vigorously - the starch and butter will emulsify into a glossy sauce that coats the pasta.
  6. Return shrimp to the pan, toss once more, season with salt and pepper. Eat immediately. Then take a nap, as Rafael did.
On pasta shape: Rafael used thin pasta and it worked fine. Thicker pasta like spaghetti or linguine holds buttery sauces better, but honestly - use what you have. The shape is less important than saving that pasta water.
~$6-8 · serves 2
Pasta · Shrimp · Nap required

Recipe 04

Loaded Baked Potato

leftover archaeology

Real

"A baked potato never sounded better... with some of yesterday's leftovers (woohoo leftovers!) You gotta bake them for pretty long, or is my oven old? IDK"

Ingredients

  • 1 large russet potato
  • Butter
  • Salt & pepper
  • Whatever leftovers are in the fridge - anything goes

Steps

  1. Heat oven to 400°F. Scrub the potato. Poke it 10-12 times with a fork all over - this lets steam out so it doesn't explode. (Yes it can explode. Yes this has happened to people. Poke the potato.)
  2. Rub with oil and a pinch of salt. Place directly on the oven rack - not a pan. The air circulation around it is what gives you a properly crispy skin.
  3. Bake 50-60 min. Test by squeezing with a towel - it should give completely with no firm spots inside. If your oven is old (Rafael's might be), give it 65 min. Undercooked potato is one of cooking's great disappointments.
  4. Reheat leftovers in a pan or microwave while you wait. Almost anything works: leftover meat, curry, veggies, sauce. The potato is just a vehicle.
  5. Cut a deep cross in the top, push the four corners in to crack it open dramatically. Add butter. Watch it melt. This is the best part. Pile on leftovers.
Regarding Rafael's oven: Older ovens run cooler than the dial says. If your potatoes always take longer than expected, your oven probably runs about 25°F cold. Just budget extra time. No big deal.
~$1-3 · serves 1, cheapest meal in the book
Potato · Zero waste

Recipe 05

Chicken Pita

QFC spice aisle adventure

Real

"Greek yogurt and random spices I found at QFC, with some cheapahhh chicken!"

Ingredients

  • 2 chicken thighs (cheaper and better than breast)
  • 2 pita breads
  • ½ cup Greek yogurt
  • Garlic powder, cumin, paprika, oregano
  • Salt & pepper
  • Lemon juice (optional)
  • Cucumber or tomato (optional)

Steps

  1. Mix all the spices together and coat the chicken all over. Let it sit 10 min minimum - even a short marination time makes a real difference. Longer is better but 10 min is fine.
  2. Heat oil in a pan on medium-high. Cook chicken 5-6 min per side without moving it. The crust needs time to form. Resist the urge to poke it every 30 seconds.
  3. Chicken is done when the thickest part hits 165°F internally, or when you cut into it and the juices run clear (no pink). Let it rest 3-4 min before slicing or it'll bleed out on the board.
  4. Make the yogurt sauce: mix Greek yogurt with a pinch of garlic powder, salt, and lemon juice if you have it. That's it. It's that easy and it's really good.
  5. Warm pita in a dry pan 30 seconds per side. Spread sauce inside, add sliced chicken, and any veg you grabbed. Roll or fold. Eat standing up over the sink if you're in a hurry.
Thighs are the superior cheap cut: More fat = more flavor = harder to overcook. If you leave them in 2 min too long they're still fine. Breast? Dry as chalk. Always buy thighs on a budget.
~$6-8 · serves 2
Chicken · Greek-ish

Recipe 06

Baked Potato with Salmon

"not a lot of ideas today" energy

Real

"Not a lot of ideas today, so I decided to make a potato again, but with salmon on it this time."

Ingredients

  • 1 large russet potato
  • 1 small salmon fillet (or canned salmon - totally valid)
  • Butter
  • Salt, pepper, lemon if available
  • Sour cream (optional)

Steps

  1. Bake potato exactly like Recipe 04. You know the drill: poke it, oil it, rack it, 400°F, 50-60 min, squeeze test. Rafael has done this before. So have you now.
  2. Season salmon with salt, pepper, and lemon juice. Heat butter in a pan on medium. Add salmon skin-side up and cook 3-4 min until golden. Flip and cook another 3 min until the flesh flakes when pressed.
  3. If using canned salmon: drain it, pick out any big bones, flake with a fork, season with lemon and pepper. Heat in a pan for 2 min. Done. Nothing wrong with canned - it's cheaper and still great.
  4. Open the potato dramatically (cross cut, push corners in). Butter, salt, pepper. Lay salmon on top. Add a dollop of sour cream if you have it. Eat and feel surprisingly satisfied for someone who "had no ideas today."
Canned salmon is underrated: It costs about $2, lasts forever in the pantry, and has the same nutrition as fresh. The flavor is milder but when it's buried in butter on a potato, nobody's complaining.
~$4-6 · serves 1
Potato · Salmon · Repeat

Recipe 07

Meatball Pasta

fridge meatballs, jar sauce, no shame

Real

"Pasta is very yummy! and cheap! and hot! help ahhhh! I made it using random meatballs in fridge and some sauce yay!"

Ingredients

  • 2 portions pasta (any shape)
  • 6-8 pre-made meatballs
  • 1 cup jarred marinara
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp butter or olive oil
  • Salt, dried oregano
  • Parmesan (optional)

Steps

  1. Salt your pasta water until it tastes like mild seawater. This is the only shot you have at seasoning the pasta itself. Don't skip it.
  2. Cook pasta 1-2 min less than the box says. It'll finish in the sauce. Save a cup of pasta water before draining.
  3. Sauté garlic in butter 30 seconds. Add meatballs, brown on all sides, 5-7 min. Pour in the sauce, add oregano, simmer 3 min.
  4. Add pasta to the sauce. Toss. Add pasta water splashes until the sauce is glossy and coats everything. Top with parmesan. Eat while hot. Yay!
Pasta water is magic: The dissolved starch makes jarred sauce silky and clingy instead of watery and sad. Save it every time without exception.
~$4-6 · serves 2
Pasta · Meatball · 20 min

Recipe 08

BBQ Pork Sandwich

pulled & sauced with quick slaw

Real

"Got a big chunk of pork, tossed with some spices and sauce with cabbage and a hamburger bun!"

Ingredients

  • ½ lb pork shoulder (cheapest cut, best results)
  • 2 tbsp BBQ sauce
  • Garlic powder, paprika, cumin, salt, pepper
  • 1 cup shredded cabbage
  • 1 tbsp mayo or Greek yogurt
  • 1 tsp apple cider vinegar, pinch of sugar
  • 2 hamburger buns

Steps

  1. Rub spices all over pork chunks. Sear on all sides in a hot oiled pan until deeply browned - 2-3 min per side. Don't rush this. The brown is flavor.
  2. Add ¼ cup water, cover, reduce to medium-low. Cook 20-25 min until the pork falls apart when you poke it. Add more water if it dries out.
  3. Shred pork with two forks. Return to pan with juices and BBQ sauce, stir on low 2 min until sticky and glazed.
  4. Quick slaw: mix cabbage, mayo, vinegar, sugar, salt. Let sit 5 min - the acid softens it from "raw and aggressive" to "pleasant and slightly tangy."
  5. Toast buns cut-side down in the empty pan. Load with pork then slaw. Press down. Eat messily.
Shoulder over everything: Pork shoulder is cheap, fatty, and gets tender with slow cooking. Lean cuts dry out completely. Buy shoulder.
~$7-9 · serves 2
Pork · Sandwich

Recipe 09

Cold Sesame Noodles

with cucumber, tomato & fried egg

Real

"Classic dish, noodles sesame sauce, cucumber, tomatoes, and sesame sauce with a fried egg and some chicken."

Ingredients

  • 2 portions thin wheat noodles
  • Cooked chicken, shredded
  • ½ cucumber, julienned
  • 1 tomato, sliced
  • 1 egg (fried)
  • 3 tbsp sesame paste or tahini
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tsp sugar
  • Warm water to thin the sauce

Steps

  1. Cook noodles. Drain and rinse under cold water until they're genuinely cold. Toss with a drop of oil. Cold noodles hold the sauce better and don't go mushy.
  2. Sesame sauce: whisk sesame paste, soy sauce, vinegar, and sugar. Add warm water 1 tbsp at a time until it's pourable - like a thick dressing. It'll seize up with cold water, so use warm.
  3. Fry the egg: medium heat, oil, crack in gently, cover 2 min. Runny yolk. Non-negotiable.
  4. Pour sauce over noodles, toss well. Top with cucumber, tomato, chicken, and the egg. Drizzle chili oil if you have it.
Sesame paste seizes in cold liquid: Always whisk with warm water. It goes from a stubborn clump to a smooth sauce immediately. This is the one technique that makes or breaks this dish.
~$6-8 · serves 2
Noodles · Cold · Sesame

Recipe 10

Tomato & Egg Noodles

simple, fast, actually great

Real

"You basically put noodles and tomato and scrambled egg together, it makes dish! Yay!"

Ingredients

  • 2 portions noodles
  • 2 large tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 3 eggs, beaten
  • 2 tbsp oil, 1 tsp sugar, 1 tbsp soy sauce, salt
  • Green onion to garnish

Steps

  1. Cook noodles, drain, toss with a drop of oil. Set aside.
  2. High heat, 1 tbsp oil. Pour in eggs - they should puff up immediately. Scramble gently, leaving them slightly underdone and custardy. Remove and set aside.
  3. Add another tbsp oil. Stir-fry tomatoes on high 2-3 min until they release their juice and soften. Add sugar and soy sauce. Stir - the sugar rounds out the acidity.
  4. Return eggs to pan. Fold in gently - you want chunks of egg, not mush. Add noodles, toss on high 1-2 min. Plate. Green onion on top.
The key is cooking them separately: Egg and tomato in the pan at the same time = rubbery scrambled egg stew. Separately then combined = two distinct textures that complement each other.
~$2-3 · serves 2 · cheapest cooked meal in the book
Noodles · Vegetarian · 15 min

Recipe 11

Upgraded Instant Noodles

with crispy spam & jammy egg

Real

"waddu expect? iz noodles! instant noodles! With eggy and spam!"

Ingredients

  • 1 packet instant ramen
  • 2 thick slices Spam
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sesame oil
  • Green onion (optional, for looking fancy)

Steps

  1. Soft-boil egg: boiling water, lower in gently, cook exactly 7 min, transfer immediately to cold water. Set a timer. This is not a step to freestyle.
  2. Dry pan, medium-high heat. Spam has its own fat so no oil needed. Sear slices 2 min per side until a dark golden crust forms. Flip once. Do not move them around. Crust requires patience.
  3. Cook ramen noodles in the same pot as the egg water. Before draining, keep ½ cup broth. Add only half the flavor packet - full packet is aggressively salty. Add sesame oil and soy sauce instead.
  4. Bowl: noodles, spam on top, egg halved (golden jammy yolk should ooze slightly), green onion. This took 15 min and looks like restaurant food. Tell no one how easy it was.
Why half the flavor packet? Full packet + soy sauce = sodium overload. Half packet + sesame oil + soy sauce = actually balanced and good. Same concept as instant ramen in every college dorm, elevated slightly.
~$3-4 · serves 1
Noodles · Spam · 15 min

Recipe 12

Mango Smoothie

peak minimalism

Real

"blender + mango + milk = mango smoothie"

Ingredients

  • 1 large mango (or 1½ cups frozen)
  • 1 cup milk (any kind)
  • ½ cup ice
  • Pinch of salt (yes really)
  • 1 tsp sugar (if mango isn't sweet)

Steps

  1. Cut mango: stand upright, slice down both sides of the pit. Score the flesh in a grid, scoop with a spoon. You will get mango juice on your shirt. Accept this.
  2. Everything in the blender. Blend 45-60 seconds on high. If it won't blend, add more milk, not more effort.
  3. Taste. Adjust sweetness. Pour. Drink before ice melts or it becomes watery mango sadness.
This counts as a meal: Rafael logged this as his meal for the day and honestly, on a hot day, it's valid. Mango has fiber, vitamins, and costs about $1.50. Nutritionally defensible. Delicious. Efficient.

Recipe 13

Boiled Pork Dumplings

boiled. not pan-fried. boiled.

Real

"pork dumpling! with veggies (mushroom, etc.)"

Ingredients

  • ½ lb ground pork
  • 4-5 shiitake mushrooms, finely diced
  • ½ cup napa cabbage, minced & squeezed dry
  • 2 green onions, minced
  • 20-25 round dumpling wrappers
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp ginger, 2 cloves garlic
  • Soy sauce + rice vinegar for dipping

Steps

  1. Mince everything tiny. Squeeze all water out of the cabbage with your hands - seriously squeeze it. Wet filling = soggy dumplings = disappointment.
  2. Mix pork with all fillings and seasoning, stirring in one direction until it feels sticky. This binds it. It looks weird. Make peace with this.
  3. Wrapper flat, wet the edge, 1 tsp filling in the center. Fold and seal. Pleat the edge by pinching small folds across the top. First few will be ugly. They still taste the same.
  4. Rolling boil. Drop dumplings in. Stir immediately so they don't stick to the bottom. When they float, add ½ cup cold water. Let it reboil.
  5. Add cold water once more. When they float again with translucent wrappers - about 7-8 min total - they're done. Slotted spoon only. Do not colander these. You'll lose too many.
  6. Dip in soy sauce + rice vinegar. Eat all 25 by yourself. No one is judging you.
The two cold water trick: Adding cold water twice during boiling slows the cook just enough for the pork to fully cook through without the wrapper going tough or bursting. Traditional method. It works.
~$7-9 · makes 20-25 dumplings
Pork · Chinese-style · Worth it

Recipe 14

Fried Rice - Fridge Cleanout Edition

leftover rice + whatever's dying in the vegetable drawer

Invented

"[no documentation - Rafael was probably too hungry to blog"

Ingredients

  • 2 cups day-old rice (leftover rice is mandatory - fresh rice makes mushy fried rice)
  • 2 eggs
  • Any vegetables that need to be used: carrots, peas, corn, green onion, literally anything
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • Oil for frying

Steps

  1. Get your pan or wok screaming hot. This is not a medium-heat dish. High heat is what gives fried rice that slightly smoky, restaurant flavor. If your smoke alarm goes off, that means you're doing it right. Probably.
  2. Add oil, then garlic. Stir 20 seconds. Add any vegetables that need longer to cook (carrots, hard veg) first, softer stuff (corn, peas, green onion) last.
  3. Push everything to the side. Crack eggs into the empty space and scramble quickly, breaking them up as they set. Fold into the vegetables before they're fully cooked.
  4. Add cold leftover rice. Break up any clumps. Toss everything on high heat, pressing the rice against the hot pan to get some crispy bits.
  5. Add soy sauce and sesame oil. Toss for another minute. Taste, adjust seasoning. Eat immediately out of the pan if you want to minimize dishes - a valid choice.
Day-old rice is not optional: Fresh rice has too much moisture and steams in the pan instead of frying. Cold rice from yesterday gets crispy and separate. If you only have fresh rice, spread it on a tray and refrigerate 1 hour. Still not as good but workable.
~$2-4 · serves 1-2
Rice · Egg · Fridge archaeology

Recipe 15

Scrambled Egg Toast

a meal so simple it's almost an insult. and yet.

Invented

"it was late, the fridge was empty, there were eggs"

Ingredients

  • 3 eggs
  • 2 tbsp butter (not oil - butter)
  • 2 slices bread, toasted
  • Salt & pepper
  • Splash of milk or water
  • Hot sauce (optional)
  • Cheese if available (optional)

Steps

  1. Crack eggs into a bowl. Add a splash of milk or water (water makes them fluffier, milk makes them richer - either works). Season with salt and pepper. Beat until completely uniform - no streaks of white remaining.
  2. Melt butter in a small pan on LOW heat. Not medium. Low. Most people cook scrambled eggs too hot, which makes them rubbery and weepy. Low and slow is the move.
  3. Pour in eggs. Stir slowly and continuously with a spatula, folding rather than scraping. Move the curds around gently. Never stop moving them.
  4. Pull the pan off heat when the eggs are still slightly underdone and glossy-looking. They'll continue cooking from residual heat. Slide onto toast immediately.
  5. Add hot sauce, cheese, or whatever you want on top. Eat. Acknowledge that eggs are one of the most underrated foods on earth.
Low heat is the secret: High heat scrambled eggs are bouncy and watery. Low heat scrambled eggs are soft, creamy, and custardy. Same ingredients, completely different result. Patience is the only technique.
~$1-2 · serves 1 · absolute floor of budget cooking
Egg · Toast · 8 min

Recipe 16

Soy Butter Noodles

three ingredients, genuinely delicious, suspiciously easy

Invented

"Rafael staring at the pantry, having a revelation"

Ingredients

  • 2 portions any noodles or spaghetti
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 clove garlic, minced (optional)
  • Green onion, sesame seeds (optional toppings)
  • Fried egg on top (highly recommended)

Steps

  1. Cook noodles. Save some pasta water before draining. This is a habit now. You do this automatically. Good.
  2. In the same hot pot, melt butter. If using garlic, add it now and cook 30 seconds until fragrant. Add soy sauce - it'll sizzle and reduce slightly for about 1 min.
  3. Add drained noodles and toss to coat. If it seems dry, add a splash of pasta water. The butter and soy sauce should glaze every strand.
  4. Top with green onion, sesame seeds, and a fried egg if you want to make it a full meal. Eat immediately. Marvel at how something this simple tastes this good.
Why this works: Soy sauce is salty, savory, and slightly sweet. Butter is fat and richness. Together they coat noodles in something that tastes like it took much more effort than it did. This is the power of umami.
~$1-3 · serves 2
Noodles · 10 min · Deceptively good

Recipe 17

Canned Tuna Rice Bowl

the staple of people who are keeping it together (barely)

Invented

" QFC, $1.29 canned tuna, moment of clarity"

Ingredients

  • 1 can tuna in water (~$1.29)
  • 1 cup cooked rice
  • 1 tbsp mayo
  • 1 tsp soy sauce
  • ½ tsp sesame oil
  • Green onion, sliced
  • Cucumber slices or any veg
  • Furikake or sesame seeds (optional)

Steps

  1. Drain the tuna. Flake it into a bowl. Add mayo, soy sauce, and sesame oil. Mix until combined. Taste it. Adjust mayo and soy to your liking. This is technically your sauce now.
  2. Cook rice (or use leftover rice - even better). Scoop into a bowl while still warm.
  3. Pile tuna mixture on top. Add sliced cucumber or any vegetables you have. Sprinkle furikake or sesame seeds if available.
  4. Mix it all together before eating, or eat in separate bites for "bowl integrity." Both are valid life choices.
Canned tuna in water vs oil: In water is cheaper and more versatile - you add your own fat (the mayo). In oil is richer but pricier. For a budget rice bowl, water-packed is the move.
~$2-4 · serves 1
Rice · Tuna · Pantry meal

Recipe 18

Quesadilla

cheese, tortilla, heat. that's it. that's the recipe.

Invented

" Rafael discovers the quesadilla at 10pm, changes as a person"

Ingredients

  • 2 flour tortillas
  • ½ cup shredded cheese (any kind)
  • Any leftover meat or veg (optional)
  • Salsa for dipping
  • Sour cream (optional)
  • Butter or oil for the pan

Steps

  1. Heat a pan on medium. Add a tiny bit of butter or oil and swirl to coat. Place one tortilla flat in the pan.
  2. Spread cheese evenly over the whole tortilla. Add any fillings on one half only - this makes it easier to fold. Don't overstuff or nothing will stay inside when you flip it.
  3. Fold the empty half over the filled half. Press down gently with a spatula. Cook 2-3 min until the bottom is golden and the cheese is visibly melting.
  4. Flip carefully. Cook another 2 min on the other side. Slide onto a cutting board and cut into triangles with a knife or scissors. Yes scissors. It works.
  5. Serve with salsa. Eat while hot, before the cheese re-solidifies into a disc. This is a time-sensitive operation.
Why medium heat: High heat = burnt tortilla, unmelted cheese in the middle. Medium heat = golden outside, fully melted inside. The tortilla and cheese need to finish at the same time.
~$2-4 · serves 1
Cheese · 10 min · Reliable

Recipe 19

Egg Fried Rice Upgrade

with frozen peas and soy sauce - a classic

Invented

" Rafael, a bag of frozen peas, and leftover rice from Day 1's curry"

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cold leftover rice
  • 2 eggs
  • ½ cup frozen peas (no need to thaw)
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • Oil for frying
  • Green onion to serve

Steps

  1. Screaming hot pan. Oil. Garlic - stir fast, 20 seconds. Add frozen peas straight from the freezer, stir-fry 2 min until bright green and thawed.
  2. Push everything to the side. Crack eggs into the empty space. Break the yolks and scramble quickly, folding the curds before they fully set. Mix into the peas and garlic.
  3. Add cold rice. Break up every clump - wet clumps steam instead of fry. Press rice against the hot pan for a few seconds at a time to get slightly crispy bits. These bits are everything.
  4. Add soy sauce around the edge of the pan (not on the rice directly - the pan edge is hotter and the soy sauce caramelizes slightly before hitting the rice). Add sesame oil. Toss 1 min. Taste. Adjust. Serve with green onion.
Soy sauce on the pan edge trick: Pouring soy sauce directly on rice makes it steam. Pouring it around the hot edge lets it sizzle and reduce slightly, concentrating the flavor before it hits the rice. Tiny difference, noticeably better result.

Teriyaki Chicken Rice Bowl

homemade teriyaki, which is just three things

Invented

Ingredients

  • 2 chicken thighs, boneless
  • 1 cup rice

  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • Oil for pan
  • Green onion and sesame to finish

Recipe 27

Pan-Fried Tofu with Soy Glaze

Rafael tries tofu. Rafael is surprised.

Invented

" a $1.99 block of firm tofu, a moment of openness, a new protein source discovered"

Ingredients

  • 1 block firm tofu (~$2)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp honey or sugar
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • Oil for frying (generous amount)
  • Rice or noodles to serve with
  • Green onion and sesame seeds

Steps

  1. Press the tofu: wrap the block in paper towels and place something heavy on top - a pan, a book, whatever's around. Press 15-20 min. This removes moisture, which is the difference between crispy tofu and sad soggy tofu. Do not skip this.
  2. Cut pressed tofu into cubes or rectangles. Pat dry one more time. They need to be as dry as possible before hitting the oil.
  3. Heat a generous amount of oil in a pan on medium-high. More oil than you think - you want the tofu to sear, not steam. Add tofu in a single layer. Do not touch it for 3-4 min until the bottom is deeply golden. Flip each piece individually. Sear the other sides.
  4. Mix soy sauce, honey, garlic powder. When tofu is golden all over, pour the sauce in. It'll bubble and caramelize around the tofu in about 1-2 min. Toss to coat. Add sesame oil at the end.
  5. Serve over rice. Top with green onion and sesame. Acknowledge that tofu, properly cooked, is actually excellent. Tell your friends.
Why tofu gets a bad reputation: Most people cook it wrong - not pressed, not dried, not enough oil, not enough heat. Soggy tofu is a technique problem, not a tofu problem. Pressed + dry + hot oil = crispy exterior, custardy inside. Completely different food.

Recipe 28

Omurice

ketchup fried rice inside an egg blanket. it's real.

Invented

" Rafael watches a Japanese cooking video at midnight and sets an alarm to make this tomorrow"

Ingredients

  • 1 cup leftover rice
  • 2 eggs
  • 3 tbsp ketchup (yes, ketchup)
  • Any leftover protein or veg (optional)
  • ½ small onion, diced
  • Butter
  • Salt & pepper
  • Extra ketchup for on top

Steps

  1. Make ketchup fried rice: sauté onion in butter 3 min. Add any leftover protein. Add cold rice, break up clumps, stir-fry 2 min. Add ketchup and toss until the rice is evenly pink and slightly caramelized. Season with salt and pepper. Set aside, keep warm.
  2. Beat 2 eggs with a pinch of salt. Heat a small non-stick pan on medium-low with butter - coat the whole surface.
  3. Pour in eggs. Swirl to coat the whole pan. Let it set very gently without scrambling - you want a thin, flat omelette that's just barely cooked. This takes patience and low heat.

  5. Squirt ketchup on top in whatever pattern feels right. A heart if you're feeling sentimental. A line if you're a minimalist. Whatever. Eat.
On the ketchup: Yes it sounds wrong. Yes it's correct. Ketchup fried rice (chicken rice) is the traditional filling for omurice and it works because ketchup has tomato, sugar, and vinegar - it seasons and glazes the rice simultaneously. Trust the process.
